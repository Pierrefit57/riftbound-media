<p class="mb-2"><b>101.</b> Deck Construction</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>102.</b> Riftbound is a Trading Card Game where a player must provide their own cards to play against other players.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>103.</b> To play Riftbound, a player must have two Decks, a Champion Legend, and a number of Battlefields determined by the Mode of Play.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>103.1.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>103.1.a.</b> 1 Champion Legend</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>103.1.b.</b> This is placed in the Legend Zone at the start of the game.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.1.b.1.</b> This will dictate the Domain Identity of the Main Deck.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.1.b.2.</b> Cards included in your Deck must abide by your Domain Identity. Your deck's Domain Identity is dictated by the domains of your Champion Legend. In the default card frame, these domains appear as symbols in the legend's upper</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.1.b.3.</b> left corner.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.1.b.4.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>103.2.</b> If a card has a single Domain, then that card is permitted in the Domain Identity</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>103.2.a.</b> that corresponds to the same Domain.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.2.a.1.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.2.a.2.</b> If a card has more than one Domain, then that card is permitted only in a Domain Identity that contains all of the indicated Domains on that card.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.2.a.3.</b> A Main Deck of at least 40 cards 1 Chosen Champion Unit Units Gear Spells</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>103.2.b.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.2.b.1.</b> Chosen Champion</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.2.b.2.</b> This will be placed in the Champion Zone at the start of the game.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>103.2.c.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>103.2.d.</b> Must be a champion unit with a champion tag that matches the tag on your</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.2.d.1.</b> Champion Legend. Example: Loose Cannon has the tag Jinx. Therefore, a player</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.2.d.2.</b> could choose Jinx, Rebel or Jinx, Demolitionist as their Chosen Champion, because</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.2.d.3.</b> they also have the tag Jinx. Example: Tibbers has the tag Annie, but it is a signature</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>103.2.e.</b> unit, not a champion unit. It cannot be your Chosen Champion, even if your</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>103.2.e.1.</b> Champion Legend has the tag Annie.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>103.3.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>103.3.a.</b> A player's Chosen Champion is both the specific card chosen for this slot during Deck Building and also any Champion Unit with the same name as the specific card selected for this specific slot during the course of play. Example: A player chose Jinx, Rebel as their Chosen Champion. One copy of Jinx, Rebel starts the game in their Chosen Champion zone. Any additional copies of Jinx, Rebel in their Main Deck, their hand, their trash, or on the board also count as their Chosen Champion for any game rules and effects that care about Chosen Champion status during the game. Your Main Deck can include up to 3 copies of the same named card. This includes your Chosen Champion. Example: A deck could include Volibear, Furious as its Chosen Champion and still include 2 more copies of Volibear, Furious. Cards have different names even if they represent the same character. Example: A deck could include 3 copies of Yasuo, Remorseful and 3 copies of Yasuo, Windrider, because they have different names. Subject to Domain Identity. Your deck may only contain 3 total Signature cards that have the same Champion tag as your Champion Legend. Regardless of name, a deck may only contain a sum total of 3 Signature cards. All of the Signature cards must have the Champion tag that corresponds to the Champion Legend of the deck. Signature cards are not Champion units and cannot be placed in the Champion Zone. During Gameplay, the Main Deck is Secret Information. It cannot be looked through or revealed unless instructed by a card effect. Rune Deck 12 Rune Cards</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>105.</b> Setup</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>106.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>106.1.</b> Spaces</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>106.2.</b> The Play Area includes a space for Game Objects referred to as The Board.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.2.a.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.2.b.</b> The Board is divided into several Zones.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.2.c.</b> The Base</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.2.d.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.2.e.</b> One per player.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>106.3.</b> The space each player always has access to play their Units and Gear to.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.3.a.</b> Each Base is a Location. Other players cannot have Game Objects they control in another player's Base.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.3.b.</b> The Base also houses each player's Runes that are on The Board.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.3.c.</b> The Battlefield Zone</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>106.4.</b> Multiple Battlefields will be here, typically one per player, but occasionally less.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.4.a.</b> The space where Battlefields are located during gameplay.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.4.b.</b> Each Battlefield is individually a Location.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.4.c.</b> The Facedown Zones Each Battlefield is associated with a sub-zone called a Facedown Zone, a single logical</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.4.d.</b> space that a card can be put facedown in through various Game Effects.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.4.e.</b> Each Facedown Zone has a maximum occupancy of one card. Cards can only be placed in or occupy the Facedown Zone if the controller of the card also controls the associated Battlefield. If the Hidden card's controller loses Control of the Battlefield, the card in the Facedown Zone is removed during the next Cleanup. See rule 318. Cleanups for more information. See rule 408. Hide for more information. Facedown Zones are not locations.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>106.5.</b> The Legend Zone</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.5.a.</b> The space for each player's Champion Legend.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.5.b.</b> This is not a location.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.5.c.</b> The Champion Legend here is a Game Object. See rule 119. Game Objects for more information.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>106.5.d.</b> The Champion Legend cannot be removed, moved, or displaced from this zone.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>107.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>107.1.</b> The Play Area also includes Non-Board Zones.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.1.a.</b> The Trash This space is where cards are placed when they are killed or discarded, when they finish</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.1.b.</b> being executed as spells, when they are moved to the trash as an effect, etc.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.1.c.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.1.d.</b> Represents cards that have been used or spent. Each player has a separate trash.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.1.e.</b> A card owned by a player can never, for any reason, be put into a trash of another player. If it would be, it's put into its owner's trash instead.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.1.f.</b> The cards in the Trash are unordered. Their sequence does not matter, and they may be</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>107.2.</b> reorganized.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.2.a.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.2.b.</b> The cards in any player's Trash are Public Information. See rule 127. Privacy for more information.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>107.2.b.1.</b> The Champion Zone</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.2.c.</b> The space each player puts their Chosen Champion at the start of the game. The Chosen Champion here cannot be returned to this zone by normal means.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>107.3.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.3.a.</b> If a Chosen Champion is instructed to be returned to this zone, it can only do so if</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.3.b.</b> there is not a card already in this zone.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.3.c.</b> The Chosen Champion can be played from here as normal, following the rules of Playing a</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>107.4.</b> Card.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.4.a.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.4.b.</b> See rule 346. Playing Cards for more information. The Main Deck Zone The space for the player's Main Deck. Primarily houses the face-down deck during gameplay. The order of cards in the deck is Secret Information during the course of play. See rule 127. Privacy for more information. The Rune Deck Zone The space for the player's Rune Deck. Primarily houses the face-down runes during gameplay.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.4.c.</b> The order of runes in the deck is Secret Information during the course of play. See rule 127. Privacy for more information.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>107.5.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.a.</b> Banishment</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.b.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.c.</b> This space is where cards are placed when they are banished by a spell or effect.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.d.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.e.</b> Represents cards that have been removed from play in a more difficult-to-recover way, or a</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.f.</b> temporary space to hold cards while effects are being processed.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.g.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.h.</b> Each player has a separate banishment.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.i.</b> A card owned by a player can never, for any reason, be put into the banishment of another</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.5.j.</b> player. If it would be, it's put into its owner's banishment instead.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>107.6.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.6.a.</b> The cards in the banishment are unordered. Their sequence does not matter, and they may</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.6.b.</b> be reorganized.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.6.c.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.6.d.</b> The cards in any player's Banishment are Public Information.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.6.e.</b> See rule 127. Privacy for more information.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>107.6.f.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>108.</b> Some spells or effects may reference cards placed into banishment directly.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>108.1.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>108.2.</b> Effects that reference cards in banishment only do so by means of also putting the cards there.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>109.</b> The effects or references to cards in banishment do not count as Choosing. The effects or references to cards in banishment do not count as Attachment. The Hand Each player has a collection of cards they may Play cards from. This is the location cards go when Drawn. The cards in a player's hand are Private Information. The number of cards in a player's hand is Public Information. The hand can be targeted as a zone, and cards there can be affected by spells and effects when specified. A card owned by a player can never, for any reason, be put into the hand of another player. If it would be, it's put into its owner's hand instead. All Game Objects in the collective Play Areas are Public Information. Any player may view, or be provided with the details of the face-up information printed on the cards in the Play Area. The State of all Game Objects in the Play Area is also Public Information. Examples: If a Unit is Buffed If a Gear is Exhausted Etc. Whenever a Game Object changes zones to or from a Non-Board Zone, all Temporary Modifications of all kinds cease to be tracked on it in all capacities. Examples: Damage is cleared. Buffs are removed.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>115.1.b.1.</b> Turn Order is established as a repeating set of the players.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>115.1.c.</b> The Mode of Play will specify how to determine a First Player.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>116.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>117.</b> The First Player is the player who becomes the Turn Player first.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>117.1.</b> Turn Order repeats until the game ends.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>117.2.</b> See the selected Mode of Play for any adjustments to each player's first turn.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>117.3.</b> See rule 458. Modes of Play for more information. Players each draw 4.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>118.</b> In turn order, players perform their Mulligan. A player may choose up to two cards in their hand. They set those cards aside.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>119.</b> Then, that player draws as many cards as they set aside. Finally, that player Recycles the cards that were set aside.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>120.</b> See rule 403. Recycle for more information. Begin play with the First Player taking their turn.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>122.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>123.</b> A Game Object is any game piece that can produce one or more Game Effects or grant prerequisites for players to take Game Actions. A Game Object does not include nor preclude any inherent properties beyond the ability to produce, or act as the prerequisite for, Game Effects and Game Actions. A Game Object can be a literal object or a logical object. Game Objects include, but are not limited to, the following: Main Deck cards in any zone Runes in any zone Legends</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>126.1.</b> Cards</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>127.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>127.1.</b> Riftbound games are composed of players playing the cards in their respective decks.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>127.2.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>127.3.</b> Ownership</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>127.4.</b> For gameplay purposes, a card's Owner is the player who brought it into the game, either as their</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>127.5.</b> Champion Legend, one of their Battlefields, or part of their Main Deck or Rune Deck. The legal</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>128.</b> owner of a card doesn't matter during gameplay.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>128.1.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>128.2.</b> Example: Bo didn't bring a deck, so Alice loans him a deck. During the game, Bo is</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>128.3.</b> considered the owner of those cards, even though legally they belong to Alice.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>128.4.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>129.</b> Privacy</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>129.1.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>129.2.</b> A card will always exist in one of the many Zones described during Setup.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>129.3.</b> A card will have a different level of Privacy based on where it resides at a given time</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>129.4.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>129.5.</b> Secret: This level of Privacy indicates that neither player may read or look at the face of the card. Private: This level of Privacy indicates that only the controller of a card on the board or the owner of a card in any other zone may read or look at the face of the card. Example: Cards in a player's hand are owned by that player. Only that player may read or look at their faces. Example: If a player controls a facedown card at a battlefield, that player and only that player may read or look at that card's face, regardless of who owns that card. Public: This level of Privacy indicates that any player may read or look at the face of the card. Back Side The back side of a card is the side with the Riftbound logo. There is a unique pattern of back side for each of the three categories of card: Main Deck cards, Rune Deck cards, and Battlefields and Champion Legends. The back side of a card is presented to conceal information for Private and Secret information cards. For example, cards in the Main Deck and cards in hand have their back side presented. The term facedown is used to describe the state during regular play of having a card's back side presented on the Board. A facedown card's front side is considered Private Information. Front Side The front side of a card is the opposite of its back. This is also referred to as a front face. This can be identified in situations where the back is not easily discernible by the presence of the Play Cost in the upper left corner of the card. Cards, when played, are played with their front face presented. The term face up is used to describe the state during regular play of having a card's front face presented</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>129.6.</b> Cost  Cards are considered to be Public Information while in this state, even if obscured by other cards in a pile. Example: The Trash is Public Information because all cards there have their front face presented</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>130.</b> even while stacked.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>130.1.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>130.2.</b> Main Deck cards have a Cost, listed in the upper left corner of the front face of the card.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>130.2.a.</b> See rule 161. Rune Pools for more information.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>130.3.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>130.3.a.</b> Energy Cost</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>130.3.b.</b> The numeral listed in the Cost element in the upper left corner of the card.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>130.</b> 4 Power cost</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>131.</b> The symbols, listed vertically, in the Cost element in the upper left corner of the card.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>131.1.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>131.2.</b> This section may not be present on every card.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>131.3.</b> Effects that need to determine a card's cost for any purpose always use its base cost, even if that cost</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>131.</b> 4                  is altered or ignored as the card is played.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>132.</b> Example: Sky Splitter is a spell that costs 8 Energy and says in part "This spell's Energy cost is</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>132.1.</b> reduced by the highest Might among units you control." Lux, Illuminated is a unit that says</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>132.2.</b> "When you play a spell that costs 5 or more, give me +3 [M] this turn." If a player who controls</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>132.3.</b> Lux plays Sky Splitter, Lux's ability will trigger, regardless of how much was actually paid for Sky Splitter.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>132.4.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>132.4.a.</b> Name Each card has a name that identifies it uniquely. This is usually located in the middle of the card. Cards that are printed in different languages but represent the same card are considered to have the same name for the purposes of deckbuilding and gameplay. Example: Chemtech Enforcer in English and its counterpart in Chinese are considered the same card, despite the Name element on the card reading differently. Some cards have both a short name and a subtitle. For all purposes, including rules and deckbuilding, such a card's name is "[Short Name], [Subtitle]". Example: Kai'Sa, Evolutionary and Kai'Sa, Survivor both have the short name Kai'Sa, but they have different names. You can include 3 of each in your deck under normal deckbuilding rules. If one of them is your Chosen Champion, the other is not. Category A card can have one or more Categories and Sub-Categories based on the properties of its front and back sides. These Categories and Sub-Categories dictate the behaviors of the card during play. Spells and other effects can refer to categories, sub-categories, supertypes, card types, tags, and other characteristics inclusively or exclusively. Example: A "non-unit card" is any card that is not a unit. Example: A "unit" is any game object that is a unit, regardless of any other categories it belongs to. Main Deck Cards begin the game in the Main Deck or (in the case of a Chosen Champion) the Champion Zone. Permanents</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>132.4.a.1.</b> An umbrella sub-category encompassing Main Deck Game Objects that remain on</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>132.4.a.2.</b> the board after being played.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>132.4.b.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>132.4.b.1.</b> Unit and Gear are permanent types.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>132.5.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>132.5.a.</b> Spells</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>132.5.a.1.</b> A sub-category encompassing Main Deck cards with the spell type, which do not</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>132.6.</b> remain on the board after being played.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>132.6.a.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>132.6.a.1.</b> Rune Deck Cards begin the game in the Rune Deck.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>132.6.b.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>132.6.b.1.</b> Runes</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>132.7.</b> A sub-category encompassing cards with the rune type. These are channeled</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>132.7.a.</b> rather than played. They remain on the board after being channeled, but they are not permanents, as they are not Main Deck cards.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>132.7.b.</b> Non-Deck Cards are not part of any deck and begin the game in a zone determined by their type.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>132.7.c.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>132.8.</b> Battlefields</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>132.8.a.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>177.</b> Tokens</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>178.</b> Tokens are Game Objects created by spells and abilities during play.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>178.1.</b> Tokens can be represented by anything. Printed tokens are included in Riftbound booster packs, but they are not required to play a token.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>178.2.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>178.3.</b> A token's controller is the controller of the spell or ability that created it, unless the token's type innately</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>179.</b> determines control or that spell or ability specifies that a different player is the token's controller. A token's owner is the player who controlled the effect that created it. The effect that creates a token may specify the conditions or circumstances under which it enters the board. These stipulations may alter the usual steps for playing a card. The effect may state that the token enters ready or exhausted, if that state is contrary to the default for the token's type. The effect may restrict the location to which the token may be played. The effect may grant temporary abilities or modifications to the token. Tokens are not cards.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>179.1.</b> Tokens have some properties in common with cards.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>179.1.a.</b> Tokens are played by their owner if their card type is played, following all the applicable steps for playing a card plus any restrictions or modifications from the effect that created the</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>179.1.b.</b> token.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>179.1.c.</b> See rule 346. Playing Cards for more information.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>179.1.d.</b> Token units have a Might. Tokens may have one or more tags.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>179.2.</b> Tokens have a type. They follow all rules for their type unless otherwise specified. Example: A</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>179.2.a.</b> token unit is a unit. It enters exhausted, can take the standard move action, deals damage</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>179.2.b.</b> equal to its Might in combat, is destroyed if it takes damage equal to or greater than its</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>180.</b> might, can be chosen or otherwise affected by spells or abilities that choose or affect units,</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>180.1.</b> etc.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>181.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>181.1.</b> Tokens differ from cards in some ways.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>181.2.</b> Tokens do not have costs. Tokens do not have domains.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>182.</b> Tokens are created on the board and cannot exist elsewhere.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>183.</b> If a token is put into any Non-Board Zone, it ceases to exist immediately after moving to its new zone.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.3.a.</b> A 3[M] Sprite token with Temporary is a domainless unit token with 3 Might, the Fae tag, and the</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-16"><b>184.3.a.1.</b> Temporary keyword. See rule 742. Temporary for more information. Control Control is the concept of a player having influence of a Game Object and applies differently to different card types. Battlefields Control is established over Battlefields through the course of play. Control is a binary state for Battlefields and an Identifier for players. A Battlefield is Controlled or Uncontrolled. A Battlefield is Controlled by a specific player or Controlled by no one. Control can be Contested through the course of play. Contested is a temporary status applied to the battlefield when a Unit controlled by a Player who does not currently Control that Battlefield Moves or otherwise becomes present there. Units moving to or being played to a battlefield apply Contested status if that battlefield is not already contested and that Unit's controller does not already</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.3.b.</b> control that battlefield.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.3.c.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.3.d.</b> A Battlefield remains Contested until Control is established or re-established.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.3.e.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>184.4.</b> While a Battlefield remains Contested, Control of a Battlefield cannot change.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.4.a.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.4.b.</b> The state of a Battlefield being Contested is used to determine when Combat should occur,</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.4.c.</b> when a Showdown without a Combat should occur, and when Control will change.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>184.5.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>184.6.</b> At this time Game Effects cannot reference this status.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.6.a.</b> Control is established by having Units at a Battlefield at the end of a Showdown or Combat after</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.6.b.</b> applying the contested status.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>184.6.c.</b> If a player controls Units at a Battlefield, outside of Combat, they maintain Control of that Battlefield for as long as they have Units at that Battlefield.</p>

<p class="mb-2"><b>185.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>185.1.</b> A player maintains control of a Battlefield while it is being Contested by an opponent.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>185.2.</b> </p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>185.2.a.</b> If a player has no Units at a Battlefield, they lose Control of that Battlefield immediately,</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>185.2.b.</b> unless it is Contested.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-12"><b>185.2.c.</b> Control is a constant state. Control of a Battlefield determines Control of its Abilities. While a Battlefield is Controlled, its Controller controls its Abilities. That player takes responsibility for adding them to the Chain if applicable, and makes all choices required by them unless otherwise specified. While a Battlefield is Uncontrolled, its Abilities are also Uncontrolled. The Turn Player takes responsibility for adding them to the Chain if applicable, makes all choices required by them unless otherwise specified, and is treated as their Controller if any game rule or effect requires one. Example: The Arena's Greatest is a battlefield that reads "At the start of each player's first Beginning Phase, that player gains 1 point." This ability will usually trigger while the battlefield has no controller. If it does, the Turn Player goes through the steps of adding the ability to the chain and receives priority after doing so, exactly as if they controlled the ability. "You" in a battlefield's abilities refers to the battlefield's Controller, as does the implied "you" in instructions like "draw 1." If the battlefield has no Controller, "you" refers to no one, and all such instructions are ignored. Everything Else When a player Plays a Card or other Game Object, they are established as that Game Object's Controller. For Spells, they are the Spell's Controller. That player chooses targets. That player chooses modes. That player pays costs.</p>

<p class="mb-2 pl-6"><b>185.3.</b> For Permanents and Runes, when they Enter the Board, that player is assigned as that Game Object's Controller.</p>
